## Summary

This track has the most serious alignment problems of the three splits. `SettingsService` already has a dedicated `SettingsService.test.ts` file, and `TvSearchService` does not exist as a service class in `server/src/services/` — it was removed as an orphan alias during the `chore_test_infrastructure_hardening_20260612` track. The track as defined cannot be executed as written. It needs to be either restructured into a `SettingsService` coverage audit or closed and replaced with a new track that correctly identifies the remaining untested TV-search orchestration surface in `WantedSearchService` and/or `MediaSearchService`.

---

## Spec Alignment

**Verdict: missed**

- **SettingsService:** The spec states it has no dedicated tests, but `server/src/services/SettingsService.test.ts` already exists with 8 tests covering `get`, `update`, `replace`, and error propagation through a mocked `AppSettingsRepository`.
- **TvSearchService:** The spec treats `TvSearchService` as a real service to test, but:
  - There is no `server/src/services/TvSearchService.ts` file.
  - `grep` for `class TvSearchService` returns no results in `server/src`.
  - `tech-debt.md` (2026-06-13 entry) and the archived `chore_test_infrastructure_hardening_20260612` track both state that the `TvSearchService` orphan alias was deleted.
  - TV search orchestration is now handled by `WantedSearchService` and `MediaSearchService`.
- Because one of the two target services does not exist, the acceptance criteria for `TvSearchService` (season/episode search dispatch, result aggregation, indexer error handling) cannot be met without first redefining what service is being tested.
- The `SettingsService` acceptance criteria mention "default fallback, validation, and serialization edge cases." The current `SettingsService` is a thin CRUD wrapper (23 lines) that delegates all defaults/validation/serialization to `AppSettingsRepository`; those concerns are not in the service itself. The acceptance criteria therefore describe repository-level behavior, not service-level behavior.
